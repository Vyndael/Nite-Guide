# DPS Player Guide
In an effort to get more people to play DPS, I decided to make this guide. DPS strips may also find this helpful. 

Disclaimer: This is a method that works for me and it may not work for you and that's okay. No one taught me any of this. I just figured it 
out as I played. Feel free to comment on this so I can make edits to improve it. 

The key part to being a good player is being able to adapt. Playing DPS is the hardest role to play because you rely entirely on your  
subgroup to do your job. It's important to remember not to blame your friends for things that may be out of their control though. There's
many variables to account for. 

So what's your purpose in the zerg? Demoralize and overwhelm the enemy healers with quick and efficient bursts to generate downs and more importantly secure those downs. 

Here's how to start:

***Game Settings***

You can't hit what you can't see. Turn down your graphics settings or tweak it so you see only the essential things. Cursor contrast is helpful.

Change your target settings so your AoEs can actually hit. When I used to play Necromancer, I liked using the fast range with indicator so 
I could ensure that I hit as many people as possible.

***Positioning***

This easiest way to stay alive is proper positioning. Staying on tag is so important...until your commander takes you over a cliff. As DPS, 
you need to be at the frontline and don't be shy. Your attacks need to actually hit the enemy. Learn to be aggressive while knowing your 
limits. Be familiar with what the range is on your attacks. Don't be in front of the commander. You should be moving parallel with him. You
have a dodge - use it if you need to fall back. 

[![Leap of Faith](https://img.youtube.com/vi/0W0HEmpkX3o.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0W0HEmpkX3o)

Following tag for me is easy. My eyes are on the enemy more than tag because I don't need to focus on where the commander is prior to 
engagement. Magics is good at call outs and markers that you can rely on his direction to move with the group and keep the tag in the corner of your eye. Use the mini map to help you orient yourself too. 

DPS players are the only ones besides scouts and dragon banner that have a good excuse to get off tag and that's to secure downs behind the 
group. Try not to get beyond 400 range. However, you should not linger behind the group too long. Do your best to kill the enemy with any 
ranged attacks as you move to catch up to the group.

Don't put yourself at risk for a few kills. 

Just because they're downed doesn't mean they will stay that way. If you're downed, they will rally. If there are a lot of downs behind the
group, feel free to let Magics know. He will turn the group back when he can to finish them off. 

If there's roamers, don't be tempted to chase. You're using a zerg build so you are unlikely to be able to stand up to roamers. You're not Gin. 

***Pattern Recognition***

After the first engagement with the enemy, you should have a reasonable idea of their comp depending on whether or not you survived. Did you get pulled often? Were you not able to move because you were CC'd? Did you not survive long because you died within seconds? Did you see what abilities the enemy DPS used? 

You should be able to figure out what the enemy team is capable of and adapt your playstyle. If I was getting pulled, it means I didn't have stab. This can mean that they have a lot of strips or your positioning is wrong. Getting stunned too much or dying fast can mean that our comp is incomplete or you're outnumbered. Recognizing abilities like traps means that the enemy team has Dragonhunters so it's crucial that you dodge it. 

All of this means that you have to tone down the aggressive playstyle and start playing much more carefully as there's less room for error. 

***Supporting the Team***

Surprisingly enough, yes, you can actually provide support and it's through basic communication. 

DPS are the first in line to use siege. Anyone who isn't using it should be taking out nearby oils, cannons, arrow carts, etc. Make sure you're near the group to stay healed or ask for help to take out a specific target away from tag. 

Once that is done, you just sit and wait for the walls and gates to be breached, right? Nope. Don't be useless. Get on your mount and start patrolling. This means setting a perimeter around the group, usually no farther than two Warclaw dashes away, and keep an eye out for who is coming. Remember, you can't be pulled off the mount but you can be stunned if your mount dies so don't get hit too much. Let Magics know what you see and use sniff on the Warclaw to find out where the enemy is stacked. 

Only a few DPS players should be doing this so just staying on tag and keeping an eye out is enough. 

If you're worried about being left behind because you were patrolling, click on the wall/gate being breached to keep it targeted so you know how much time you have before you need to get back on tag. 

If you see an enemy zerg, point it out and be clear about their position. Use the minimap. Call out if you see them using abilities like veil or if you see a portal. You may save the group from a wipe. 

When necessary, I will target the enemy commander to break them. This is especially helpful if you're outnumbered. Learn to figure out who is the commander. Unless Gin is there because he knows everyone. 

Once someone says dragon banner has been pulled, you need to start looking for who it is. It is your job to hunt them down before they become a big problem. 

Remain on tag! Magics knows to prioritize it so just be patient. You don't even need to kill it, just down it to make it lose the banner. If you see it, let everyone know by identifying the guild tag, race, and location. Someone will mark them to make it easier for everyone to target because if you mark, only your subgroup can see it. 

***Adaptation*** 

One thing I can't teach you to do is how to play your class well. It ultimately comes down to practice. Go hit the golem in Lion's Arch or the borderlands. 

Being a good DPS player means you have to make a lot of judgement calls. You have to predict the enemy. Look at their movement. If you can tell they're going in one direction, you can leave an AoE in the path they are going to take or even hit their tail. 

If you see that your hits are getting blocked or the enemy is invulnerable, don't waste your burst. Save it for when it can actually hit. 

[![Invulnerables & Blocks](https://img.youtube.com/vi/p762dSGg_Ws.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p762dSGg_Ws)

If they like to veil, you need to start using a ranged attack or drop an AoE nearby (either auto attack or low CD) to reveal them before
they reach you.

[![AoE Mag](https://img.youtube.com/vi/0HKG8pssGMY.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HKG8pssGMY)

If we're up against Mag, you have to pay attention and dodge their burst. It is possible as you can see it for a second or two before it
hits.

**Dragon Banner**

When the time calls for dragon banner, DPS is supposed to be the one to take the responsibility. However, I don't recommend it with our
current comp. With the way I have my Amalgam set up, I have very little survivability and the banner takes most of it away. Ideally,  
Spellbreaker would have the best chance to survive followed by Reaper. 

TLDR: Skill issue. Practice and listen to Magics. Stay on tag. Clear comms when near the enemy.
